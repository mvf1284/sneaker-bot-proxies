## What sneaker bot proxies actually do

A sneaker proxy routes a connection through another IP address. In sneaker-related workflows, people usually look for proxies because one home connection is a poor fit for running many separate, legitimate sessions or testing retail-site performance from a specific US network.

The important distinction is whether the IP remains the same.

- **Static ISP proxies** keep the assigned IP stable. They suit workflows where continuity matters across several pages or steps.
- **Rotating residential proxies** change IPs periodically or per request. They are generally better suited to broad, stateless data collection, where session continuity is less important.
- **Datacenter proxies** are often inexpensive and fast, but their network classification can make them a poor match for sites that apply stricter traffic controls.
- **Mobile proxies** use mobile-network addresses. They can be useful in narrowly defined mobile testing cases, but cost and latency are often less predictable.

For sneaker bot proxies, static ISP addresses are usually the category worth evaluating first when your use case requires a persistent US session. They are not magic keys to a drop, though. Retail platforms also evaluate account history, browser behavior, payment checks, inventory rules, request volume, and other signals. An IP address is one part of a much larger picture.

## The cost question: per IP is not the whole story

Proxy pricing can look deceptively simple. A provider might advertise a lower per-IP number but charge for traffic, reduce concurrency after a usage threshold, or assign addresses from a shared pool with a mixed history.

HypeProxies uses a per-IP model and publicly advertises unlimited bandwidth on these plans. That creates a predictable invoice: the listed plan price does not rise simply because you used more data.

That matters most for bandwidth-heavy, legitimate work. If your permitted workflow sends plenty of requests or transfers sizable page content, a per-GB model can become difficult to budget. On the other hand, if your actual usage is light and you only need a handful of IPs, a metered provider with a much smaller minimum may still cost less overall.

So compare total cost, not only the marketing headline:

- Minimum purchase size
- Per-IP price
- Traffic limits, fair-use restrictions, or overage fees
- Whether an IP is dedicated or shared
- Replacement terms
- Required protocol support
- Geographic coverage
- Contract length and cancellation conditions

The cheapest proxy on paper is rarely the cheapest proxy after it fails a compatibility check.

## A sensible pre-purchase checklist

Before buying sneaker bot proxies, answer these questions in writing. It takes five minutes and can save you from paying three months for the wrong product.

### 1. What is the allowed use case?

Read the retail platform’s terms and your software provider’s rules. Do not assume a proxy makes prohibited automation acceptable. It does not.

### 2. Do you need a stable IP or a rotating pool?

For a session-dependent process, a stable static IP may be appropriate. For a stateless permitted data task, a rotating pool may be more economical. Buying the wrong category is the most common expensive mistake.

### 3. Is the target US-only?

HypeProxies’ public ISP offering is US-focused. If your intended location is elsewhere, solve that mismatch before paying.

### 4. Does your tool support HTTP proxies?

HypeProxies publicly identifies HTTP support. Verify the exact protocol requirement in your own tool before purchase instead of treating “proxy compatible” as a complete answer.

### 5. How many IPs do you actually need?

Start from approved concurrent sessions and capacity needs, then choose the plan. Do not start from the biggest discount and invent a use for it afterward.

### 6. Are you comparing total cost?

Include billing length, bandwidth policy, support level, geographic fit, and replacement terms. A $1.30 IP with unlimited bandwidth means something very different from a $1.30 IP with a traffic limit attached.
